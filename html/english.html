SBI PO English Question Models: Complete Analysis with Examples

Based on Memory-Based Papers (March 2025 - August 2025)

MODEL 1: CLOZE TEST / FILL IN THE BLANKS

Definition: Questions with blanks that require filling with appropriate words based on grammar, vocabulary, and context.

Structure:

  • Single word or phrase fits the blank
  • Context clues guide the answer
  • Tests vocabulary, collocation, and grammar

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (March 2025 - Q1):

Passage: “The controlled use of fire represents one of humanity’s most _______ technological breakthroughs.”

  • (A) trivial / (B) seminal / © negligible / (D) redundant / (E) derivative
  • Answer: (B) seminal
  • Explanation: Only “seminal” (groundbreaking, important) fits the context that fire control was a major breakthrough.

Example 2 (March 2025 - Q2):

Passage: “Anthropologists believe our ancestors first ______ fire naturally caused by lightning strikes.”

  • (A) extinguished / (B) harnessed / © squandered / (D) circumvented / (E) deprecated
  • Answer: (B) harnessed
  • Explanation: “Harnessed” means controlled/used purposefully, fitting the narrative of learning to use fire.

Example 3 (August 2025 - Q10):

Passage: “The CEO’s speech was _______ with optimism and _______ a sense of hope among the employees.”

  • (A) brimming, instilled / (B) loaded, concluded / © vague, removed / (D) shallow, hampered / (E) infused, revealed
  • Answer: (A) brimming, instilled
  • Explanation: “Brimming” (full of) optimism and “instilled” (created) hope are the logical collocations.

Example 4 (August 2025 - Q11):

Passage: “The manager handled the crisis with _______ efficiency and a _______ sense of calm.”

  • (A) clumsy, fake / (B) strategic, genuine / © reckless, shallow / (D) spontaneous, erratic / (E) minimal, loud
  • Answer: (B) strategic, genuine
  • Explanation: Crisis management requires strategic approach and genuine composure, not recklessness or chaos.

Example 5 (August 2025 - Q12):

Passage: “The artist’s latest work was _______ by critics, but it still _______ a deep emotional response from viewers.”

  • (A) appreciated, conveyed / (B) acclaimed, predicted / © condemned, evoked / (D) applauded, generated / (E) honored, created
  • Answer: © condemned, evoked
  • Explanation: The conjunction “but” signals contrast—criticized yet still emotionally moving. “Evoked” (stirred up) fits perfectly.

Example 6 (August 2025 - Q13):

Passage: “Though the solution seemed _______, it had severe _______ on the long-term budget.”

  • (A) beneficial, complications / (B) simple, implications / © risky, suggestions / (D) vague, effects / (E) optimal, procedures
  • Answer: (B) simple, implications
  • Explanation: A simple solution has serious “implications” (consequences) for the budget. The “but” structure signals this contrast.

Example 7 (August 2025 - Q18):

Passage from Agriculture passage: “Agriculture has always been at the heart of human civilization, but in recent years, there has been a _______ shift toward locally grown foods.”

  • (A) anticipant / (B) subtle / © significant / (D) regularized / (E) propellant
  • Answer: © significant
  • Explanation: The shift toward local foods represents a major (“significant”) movement, not a minor one.

Example 8 (August 2025 - Q19):

Passage: “Locally grown produce often arrives fresher at markets, having skipped the _______ transportation routes associated with imported goods.”

  • (A) associate / (B) astound / © elaborate / (D) distant / (E) unified
  • Answer: (D) distant
  • Explanation: Long transportation routes are “distant,” explaining why local produce is fresher.

Example 9 (August 2025 - Q22):

Passage: “Consumer support plays a critical role—by choosing local, individuals can help _______ sustainable agriculture and reduce their environmental footprint.”

  • (A) affirm / (B) limited / © scale / (D) promote / (E) conceal
  • Answer: (D) promote
  • Explanation: Choosing local helps “promote” (advance) sustainable agriculture.

Example 10 (August 2025 - Q23):

Passage: “If this movement gains more momentum, it could _______ the future of farming into a more ethical and ecologically balanced system.”

  • (A) prevail / (B) transform / © maintain / (D) acquire / (E) duplicate
  • Answer: (B) transform
  • Explanation: “Transform” (change completely) fits the idea of shifting farming into a completely different model.

MODEL 2: ERROR SPOTTING / GRAMMATICAL ERROR IDENTIFICATION

Definition: Identifying grammatical or idiomatic errors in sentences divided into segments.

Structure:

  • Sentence divided into 4 parts: (A), (B), ©, (D)
  • One segment contains error
  • Tests: Subject-verb agreement, tense, prepositions, articles, parallelism

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (March 2025 - Q6):

Sentence: “The pack of wolves (A) were seen roaming (B) near the deserted village © at dawn. (D)”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (a) A
  • Explanation: “Pack” is singular, so it should be “was seen,” not “were seen.” Subject-verb agreement error.

Example 2 (March 2025 - Q7):

Sentence: “She narrowly escaped (A) to fall into the (B) deep trench © near the cliff’s edge. (D)”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (a) A
  • Explanation: After “escaped,” use gerund “falling,” not infinitive “to fall.” Should be “escaped falling.”

Example 3 (March 2025 - Q8):

Sentence: “Despite being warned (A) about the storm, he insisted (B) to go for a © late-night drive. (D)”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: © C
  • Explanation: After “insisted,” use gerund “on going,” not infinitive. Should be “insisted on going.”

Example 4 (March 2025 - Q9):

Sentence: “He pretended (A) that he knows nothing (B) about the secret plan © discussed in the meeting. (D)”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (b) B
  • Explanation: After “pretended” (past tense), verb should be past: “knew,” not “knows.” Tense inconsistency.

Example 5 (March 2025 - Q10):

Sentence: “Not only he (A) ignore the warnings, (B) but he also © mocked the elders. (D)”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (a) A
  • Explanation: After “Not only,” invert subject-verb: “Not only did he ignore.” Should be “Not only did he.”

Example 6 (August 2025 - Q32):

Sentence: “China’s high growth based on resource (A)/intensive manufacturing, exports, and (B)/ low-paid labor has largely reached its limits and ©/ has led to economic, social, and environmental imbalances (D).”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (e) No error
  • Explanation: Sentence is grammatically correct. Parallelism maintained: “resource-intensive manufacturing, exports, and low-paid labor.”

Example 7 (August 2025 - Q33):

Sentence: “Before the pandemic, the city (A)/ airport grappled with severe bottlenecks (B)/ as the terminals couldn’t handled ©/ the traffic, with passengers bearing the brunt of it (D).”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: © C
  • Explanation: After “couldn’t,” use base verb “handle,” not past participle “handled.” Should be “couldn’t handle.”

Example 8 (August 2025 - Q34):

Sentence: “A remedial measure to cure the (A)/ patriarchal mindset of the socio-legal system(B)/ would be the inclusion of feminist ©/jurisprudence in the curriculum by law students. (D)”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (e) No error
  • Explanation: Sentence is grammatically correct with proper parallelism and coherent structure.

Example 9 (March 2025 - Q11):

Sentence: “He was neither interested in the opera (A)/ nor he wanted (B)/ to spend money ©/ on the concert tickets. (D)/ No error”

  • (a) A / (b) B / © C / (d) D / (e) No error
  • Answer: (b) B
  • Explanation: Correlative “neither…nor” requires parallel structure. Should be “nor did he want,” not “nor he wanted.”

Example 10 (March 2025 - Q12):

Sentence: “Were she to disclose the truth, the entire committee would be in disarray.”

  • Replace “Were she to disclose” with most appropriate option
  • Best option: “If she were to disclose” (maintains conditional)
  • Explanation: Tests conditional mood; “Were she” is inverted form but needs appropriate context.

MODEL 3: SENTENCE REARRANGEMENT / PARA JUMBLES

Definition: Jumbled sentences that need to be arranged in logical sequence to form coherent paragraph.

Structure:

  • 5-6 sentences labeled A-F
  • Must identify: 1st, 2nd, 3rd, etc. sentence
  • Tests: Logical flow, connectors, pronoun references

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (March 2025 - Q26-Q30):

Given Sentences:

  • (A) Coffee prices have shown signs of stabilization as new harvests approach
  • (B) Although both beverages were affected by supply shocks, the nature of disruptions varied
  • © Meanwhile, orange juice prices spiked as Florida and Brazil battled diseases
  • (D) Over the past year, global prices of both coffee and orange juice surged significantly
  • (E) This comparative trend highlights how different agricultural vulnerabilities cause varied trajectories
  • (F) Coffee-producing regions in Brazil faced severe droughts and unexpected frosts

Sequence: D-B-F-C-A-E

  • Q31. First sentence: (d) D ✓
  • Q32. Second sentence: (b) B ✓
  • Q33. Third sentence: © F ✓
  • Q34. Fifth sentence: (d) E ✓
  • Q35. Last sentence: © E ✓
  • Logic: Introduction (D) → Explanation of variety (B) → Details on coffee (F) → Details on orange juice © → Transition (A) → Conclusion (E)

Example 2 (August 2025 - Q36-Q40):

Given Sentences:

  • (A) Over time, consistent overconsumption of sugar has been linked to chronic conditions

  • (D) Reducing sugar consumption can significantly improve overall health outcomes
  • (E) Sugar is often added to processed foods and beverages unknowingly

Sequence: F-B-E-C-A-D

  • First sentence: (e) F (Introduces modern problem)
  • Fifth sentence: (a) D (Solution presented)
  • Logic: Problem introduction → Nature of problem → Where problem exists → Concerns → Consequences → Solution

MODEL 6: PHRASE REPLACEMENT / IDIOM CORRECTION

Definition: Identifying and replacing incorrect phrases or idioms with correct alternatives.

Structure:

  • Phrase highlighted in bold
  • Must identify if correct or choose replacement
  • Tests: Idiomatic usage, prepositions, collocation

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (August 2025 - Q39):

Sentence: “Despite of the heavy storm, the fishermen decided to set sail.”

  • Replace: “Despite of” is incorrect
  • Correct options:
    • (a) Despite the heavy storm ✓
    • (b) In spite the heavy storm ✗
    • © Even despite the heavy storm ✗
    • (d) Although of the heavy storm ✗
  • Answer: (a) Despite the heavy storm
  • Explanation: “Despite” takes object directly; “of” is redundant. “Despite + noun” is correct.

Example 2 (August 2025 - Q40):

Sentence: “Had he known about the crisis, he would help his colleagues.”

  • Replace: “would help” is incorrect tense
  • Correct options:
    • (a) he would had helped ✗
    • (b) he would helped ✗
    • © he will help ✗
    • (d) he would have helped ✓
  • Answer: (d) he would have helped
  • Explanation: Third conditional requires: “Had + past participle…would have + past participle”

Example 3 (August 2025 - Q15):

Sentence: “By the time she will reach the station, the train will have left.”

  • Replace: “she will reach” is incorrect
  • Correct: “she reaches” (present tense in future clause)
  • Explanation: In “by the time” clauses, use simple present, not future.

Example 4 (August 2025 - Q16):

Sentence: “Neither of the boys have finished their homework yet.”

  • Replace: “have finished” is incorrect
  • Correct: “has finished” (singular subject “neither”)
  • Explanation: “Neither” is singular, requires singular verb “has”

Example 5 (August 2025 - Q17):

Sentence: “Had I knew about the event, I would have attended it.”

  • Replace: “knew” is incorrect
  • Correct: “had known”
  • Explanation: Conditional “Had I” requires perfect form “had known”

Example 6 (August 2025 - Q12):

Sentence: “Were she to disclose the truth, the entire committee would be in disarray.”

  • Status: Correct
  • Alternative acceptable: “If she were to disclose…”
  • Explanation: Inverted conditional with “Were” is grammatically correct.

Example 7 (August 2025 - Q14):

Sentence: “The journalist’s report was _____ researched and delivered in a _____ manner.”

  • Replace with: “thoroughly, lucid”
  • Explanation: “Thoroughly researched” (adverb + adjective) and “lucid manner” (clear communication)

Example 8 (March 2025 - Phrase Replacement):

Sentence: “He narrowly escaped to fall into the deep trench.”

  • Error: “to fall” (infinitive)
  • Correct: “falling” (gerund after “escaped”)
  • Rule: Escape + -ing form

Sentence: “Despite being warned about the storm, he insisted to go for a late-night drive.”

  • Error: “insisted to go”
  • Correct: “insisted on going”
  • Rule: Insist + on + -ing form

Example 10 (August 2025 - Sentence Substitution):

Sentence: “He narrowly escaped to be arrested by the border guards while crossing.”

MODEL 7: DOUBLE BLANK FILL / WORD PAIR SELECTION

Definition: Selecting appropriate word pairs to complete two blanks maintaining logical flow.

Structure:

  • Two blanks in single sentence or connected sentences
  • Must find pair that fits both grammatically and contextually
  • Tests: Collocations, relationships between ideas

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (August 2025 - Q12):

Passage: “The artist’s latest work was _______ by critics, but it still _______ a deep emotional response from viewers.”

  • Options:
    • (a) appreciated, conveyed
    • (b) acclaimed, predicted
    • © condemned, evoked ✓
    • (d) applauded, generated
    • (e) honored, created
  • Answer: © condemned, evoked
  • Logic: The “but” signals contrast: criticized (condemned) yet still emotionally moving (evoked). “Evoked” is more precise for stirring emotions.

Example 2 (August 2025 - Q13):

Passage: “Though the solution seemed _______, it had severe _______ on the long-term budget.”

  • Options:
    • (a) beneficial, complications
    • (b) simple, implications ✓
    • © risky, suggestions
    • (d) vague, effects
    • (e) optimal, procedures
  • Answer: (b) simple, implications
  • Logic: Though signals contrast. Simple solution = practical appearance. Implications = consequences. “Implications” is more formal for discussing budget consequences.

Example 3 (August 2025 - Q11):

Passage: “The manager handled the crisis with _______ efficiency and a _______ sense of calm.”

  • Answer: (b) strategic, genuine
  • Logic: Crisis management needs strategic approach (well-planned) and genuine composure (real, not fake). Collocations work: “strategic efficiency,” “genuine calm”

Example 4 (August 2025 - Q10):

Passage: “The CEO’s speech was _______ with optimism and _______ a sense of hope among the employees.”

  • Answer: (a) brimming, instilled
  • Logic: “Brimming with optimism” (full of) and “instilled hope” (created, cultivated). Perfect collocations.

Example 5 (August 2025 - Q13-Agriculture):

Passage: “Though the solution seemed _____, it had severe _____ on the long-term budget.”

  • Correct answer: “simple, implications”
  • Why: Solution appearing simple but having complex (implied) consequences for budget.

Example 6 (March 2025 - Q24):

Passage: “The monk’s teachings, though ancient, still _____ relevance today and continue to _____ seekers across the globe.”

  • Answer: (a) retain, inspire
  • Logic: Teachings “retain” (keep, maintain) relevance AND “inspire” (motivate) seekers. Natural collocations.

Example 7 (March 2025 - Q25):

Passage: “The army commander’s decision was _____, made without full information, and it _____ the entire mission.”

  • Answer: (a) abrupt, jeopardized
  • Logic: Decision was “abrupt” (sudden, hasty) and “jeopardized” (put at risk) the mission. Cause-effect relationship.

Example 8 (August 2025 - Q14):

Passage: “The journalist’s report was _______ researched and delivered in a _______ manner.”

  • Answer: (b) thoroughly, lucid
  • Logic: Work “thoroughly researched” (complete, detailed) and delivered with “lucid” (clear) style. Professional writing standards.

  • Answer: © blend, crafted
  • Logic: Novel is a “blend” (mixture, combination) of genres and “crafted” (carefully made) to challenge. Shows intentionality.

  • Answer: (b) foresees, taken
  • Logic: Report “foresees” (predicts) problem and steps must be “taken” (implemented, acted upon). Prediction and action relationship.

MODEL 8: SENTENCE CORRECTION / GRAMMAR SUBSTITUTION

Definition: Selecting best alternative to replace highlighted segment in sentence.

Structure:

  • Highlighted phrase/clause in sentence
  • 5 options with “No replacement required”
  • Tests: Grammar, word choice, parallelism, idiom

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (August 2025 - Q15):

Sentence: “By the time she will reach the station, the train will have left.”

  • Highlighted: “she will reach”
  • Options:
    • (a) she reaches ✓
    • (b) she had reached
    • © she would have reached the station
    • (d) she is reaching
    • (e) No replacement required
  • Answer: (a) she reaches
  • Explanation: In “by the time” clauses, use simple present tense, not future.

Example 2 (August 2025 - Q16):

Sentence: “Neither of the boys have finished their homework yet.”

  • Highlighted: “have finished”
  • Options:
    • (a) has finished his ✓
    • (b) have finished his
    • © has finished their
    • (d) has finished her
    • (e) No replacement required
  • Answer: (a) has finished his
  • Explanation: “Neither” (singular) requires “has” + singular possessive “his”

Example 3 (August 2025 - Q39):

Sentence: “Despite of the heavy storm, the fishermen decided to set sail early in the morning.”

  • Highlighted: “Despite of”
  • Options:
    • (a) Despite the heavy storm ✓
    • (b) In spite the heavy storm
    • © Even despite the heavy storm
    • (d) Although of the heavy storm
    • (e) No replacement required
  • Answer: (a) Despite the heavy storm
  • Explanation: “Despite” is preposition taking noun directly; “of” is redundant.

Example 4 (August 2025 - Q40):

Sentence: “Had he known about the crisis, he would help his colleagues before leaving.”

  • Highlighted: “he would help”
  • Options:
    • (a) he would had helped
    • (b) he would helped
    • © he will help
    • (d) he would have helped ✓
    • (e) No replacement required
  • Answer: (d) he would have helped
  • Explanation: Third conditional: “Had…would have” structure required

Example 5 (August 2025 - Q14):

Sentence: “The journalist’s report was _______ researched and delivered in a _______ manner.”

  • Most appropriate option: “thoroughly, lucid”
  • Testing: Adverb-adjective pairing, word precision

Example 6 (August 2025 - Q28):

Sentence: “Developing emotional intelligence helps in (A) building stronger conflicts (B) and managing relationships © effectively in professional (D) and personal life.”

  • Error: “conflicts” should be “connections”
  • Interchange needed: A-B (or B-A to read naturally)

Example 7 (August 2025 - Q29):

Sentence: “Effective communication (A) and a clear achieving (B) promote teamwork toward understanding © shared goals…”

  • Error: “achieving” should be where “understanding” is
  • Correction needed: B-C interchange

Example 8 (August 2025 - Q30):

Sentence: “Consistent effort (A) and disciplined practice are vital (B) for mastering complex skills ©…”

  • Status: No interchange required; already grammatically correct
  • Answer: (e) No interchange required

Example 9 (August 2025 - Q31):

Sentence: “Staying adaptable (A) and open to learning prepares (B) growth and fosters © individuals…”

  • Errors: “prepares” and “fosters” need rearrangement
  • Correction: A-C and B-D needed

  • Highlighted: “to be arrested”
  • Correct: “being arrested”

MODEL 9: CONNECTORS & LOGICAL FLOW (Sentence Rearrangement Details)

Definition: Understanding how connectors (however, therefore, moreover, although, etc.) guide logical progression.

Structure:

  • Uses connectors like: however, therefore, moreover, although, despite, whereas, etc.
  • Tests: Understanding relationships between ideas
  • Part of para jumbles and reading comprehension

10 Examples from Source Papers:

Example 1 (March 2025 - Online Betting Passage):

Connector Usage: “However, despite these apparent advantages, the rapid expansion…”

  • Function: Introduces contrasting information
  • Signals: Shift from benefits to risks

Example 2 (August 2025 - Travel Industry):

Example: “However, SunWings made a bold decision to revamp its model.”

  • Function: Contrasts potential shutdown with actual recovery strategy
  • Logic: Despite crisis → strategic response

Example 3 (August 2025 - Agricultural Passage):

Example: “Moreover, local farming strengthens the economy…”

  • Function: Addition connector - adds supporting point
  • Shows: Multiple benefits accumulated

Example 4 (March 2025 - Para Jumble):

Connector Pattern: “Although…However…Therefore…”

  • Creates: Problem statement → Complication → Solution progression

Example 5 (August 2025 - Disability Support):

Example: “However, barriers such as a shortage of trained interpreters persist.”

  • Function: Introduces challenges despite positives
  • Creates: Balanced perspective

Example 6 (March 2025 - Economic Crisis):

Connector: “Despite its widespread use, sugar provides…”

  • Function: Concessive - acknowledges point but contrasts
  • Shows: Paradox between popularity and lack of nutrition

Example 7 (August 2025 - National Strength):

Example: “As air travel remained limited, __________ regional transport options gained importance.”

  • Logic: As + condition triggers consequence
  • Connector needed: indicates cause-effect

Example 8 (Para Jumble Pattern):

Sequence: Establish (A) → Qualify/Explain (B) → Provide example © → Add detail (D) → Conclude (E-F)

  • Connectors used: Though, However, For example, Moreover, Therefore

Example 9 (Reading Comp - Inference):

Understanding implied connectors:

  • Statement 1: “The industry suffered severe losses”
  • Implied connection: THEREFORE/AS A RESULT
  • Statement 2: “Many companies faced potential shutdown”

Example 10 (Logical Progression Example):

Topic: Climate Change Impacts

  • Opening: “Climate change poses unprecedented challenges” (problem)
  • Transition: “However, some regions have adapted successfully” (counterpoint)
  • Development: “For instance, renewable energy adoption…” (example)
  • Conclusion: “Therefore, comprehensive policies are essential” (implication)

PREPARATION STRATEGY BY MODEL

For Cloze Tests:

  • Build strong vocabulary (50+ words weekly)
  • Understand collocation patterns
  • Practice context clue identification
  • Focus on prepositions and verb patterns

For Error Spotting:

  • Revise grammar rules systematically
  • Master subject-verb agreement
  • Study common idioms and prepositions
  • Practice tense consistency

For Para Jumbles:

  • Identify connector words
  • Track pronoun references
  • Understand logical progression
  • Practice sentence sequencing daily

For Reading Comprehension:

  • Skim passage first (30-45 seconds)
  • Read questions before detailed reading
  • Practice inference skills
  • Note author’s tone and purpose

  • Understand word in context, not in isolation
  • Learn multiple meanings of common words
  • Practice contextual usage
  • Build vocabulary through reading
