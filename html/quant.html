Quantitative Aptitude: Maximum Correct Attempts in Limited Time

1. Objective

This guide is designed for SBI PO and IBPS PO Prelims candidates who want to maximize correct Quant questions within the fixed sectional time.

Your target is not to solve every question. Your target is to:

Recognize quickly -> choose correctly -> calculate cleanly -> leave early when slow

For your current profile, the first improvement should come from short, repeatable topics and calculation speed. Difficult arithmetic should be added only after the easy marks become reliable.

2. Current Exam Constraint

Recent official SBI PO information lists Quantitative Aptitude as a separately timed section of 20 minutes. The latest IBPS notification must always be checked before the relevant attempt because question counts, marks, timing and rules can change.

Official references:

  • SBI PO official information handout
  • IBPS PO/MT official notification

Use the latest notification for the final pattern. The strategy below assumes a 20-minute Quant section.

3. The Core Strategy: Maximize Net Marks

Your effective score depends on correct answers minus penalties for wrong answers. Therefore:

  • Attempt a question when the method and approximate time are clear.
  • Skip a question when the setup is unclear after one reading.
  • Do not spend two minutes proving that a one-mark question is difficult.
  • Prefer 10 highly accurate attempts over 15 rushed attempts with avoidable errors.

Three-question classification

Classify every question immediately:

Green: method obvious, likely under 45 seconds -> solve now
Yellow: method known, but calculation is moderate -> return later
Red: lengthy, unfamiliar or case-heavy -> skip unless time remains

4. Recommended 20-Minute Attempt Plan

Pass 1: 0-5 minutes

Scan and solve the shortest questions:

  1. Simplification and approximation.
  2. Quadratic equations.
  3. Easy number series.
  4. Direct percentage, ratio or average questions.
  5. Very short data-interpretation questions.

Do not start a long DI set or an unfamiliar arithmetic problem in Pass 1.

Pass 2: 5-14 minutes

Take:

  • Remaining short questions.
  • Easy arithmetic with a clear equation.
  • DI questions whose table or graph is simple.
  • A second number-series or quadratic question if still pending.

Pass 3: 14-19 minutes

Return to selected yellow questions:

  • One manageable DI set.
  • One familiar arithmetic topic.
  • Questions where only calculation remains.

Final minute

  • Check marked answers for sign, unit and decimal errors.
  • Do not begin a fresh lengthy question.
  • Avoid changing an answer without a specific reason.

5. Topic Priority Ladder

Tier 1: Highest return for time

Master these first:

  • Simplification
  • Approximation
  • Quadratic equations
  • Number series
  • Percentage
  • Ratio and proportion
  • Average
  • Basic profit, loss and discount
  • Direct table or bar-graph DI

Tier 2: Important arithmetic

  • Simple interest and compound interest
  • Time, speed and distance
  • Boats and streams
  • Time and work
  • Pipes and cisterns
  • Mixture and allegation
  • Partnership
  • Data sufficiency with clear statements

Tier 3: Selective topics

Attempt these only when the structure is familiar:

  • Probability
  • Permutation and combination
  • Advanced mensuration
  • Complex trains
  • Multiple-person work and efficiency cases
  • DI with several missing values or lengthy percentages

Do not allow Tier 3 topics to reduce your accuracy in Tier 1.

6. Simplification and Approximation

Fast order of operations

Use the order:

Brackets -> powers/roots -> division/multiplication -> addition/subtraction

Fraction and percentage anchors

1/2  = 50%       1/3  = 33.33%       1/4  = 25%
1/5  = 20%       1/6  = 16.67%       1/8  = 12.5%
1/10 = 10%       1/12 = 8.33%        1/16 = 6.25%
1/20 = 5%        1/25 = 4%           1/40 = 2.5%

Approximation rules

  • Round numbers only when the answer options are sufficiently separated.
  • Keep the same rounding direction for comparable terms.
  • Do not round a denominator to zero or to a value that changes the answer region.
  • Estimate first; calculate exactly only if two options remain close.

Calculation shortcut

For percentage multiplication:

18% of 250 = 10% of 250 + 8% of 250
             = 25 + 20
             = 45

For products near a round number:

49 x 51 = (50 - 1)(50 + 1) = 50^2 - 1 = 2499

7. Quadratic Equations

If the equation is:

x^2 - 11x + 30 = 0

factor it as:

(x - 5)(x - 6) = 0

so the roots are 5 and 6.

Fast comparison rules

  • If both roots of the two equations are positive and clearly separated, compare directly.
  • If one equation has roots of different signs and the other has both roots positive, the comparison may be decided by sign.
  • If factoring is not visible in 20-25 seconds, mark it and return later.
  • Do not solve roots to unnecessary decimal precision.

Common error

Always compare both roots. A single root comparison is invalid when the question asks whether x is greater than, less than or equal to y and both equations have two roots.

8. Number Series

Check patterns in this order:

  1. First differences.
  2. Second differences.
  3. Multiplication or division.
  4. Multiplication followed by addition/subtraction.
  5. Alternating operations.
  6. Squares, cubes or prime-number additions.

Example:

3, 7, 15, 31, 63, ?

Pattern: x2 + 1
Answer: 127

Speed rule

Give a series 20-30 seconds. If no pattern appears, skip it. A series that needs a complicated invented rule is usually not worth early time.

9. Percentage, Ratio and Average

Percentage change

New value = Old value x (100 +/- change percentage) / 100

Successive changes a% and b% combine as:

Net change = a + b + (ab / 100)

Use the signs of the changes. For a rise followed by a fall, take one as positive and the other as negative.

Ratio method

If:

A:B = 3:5 and total = 64

then:

One part = 64 / 8 = 8
A = 24, B = 40

Weighted average

Use the total-value method:

Average = total quantity / number of items
Total quantity = average x number of items

When two groups are combined, calculate each group total first. Do not average the two averages unless the group sizes are equal.

10. Profit, Loss and Discount

Profit = SP - CP
Loss   = CP - SP
Profit% = Profit / CP x 100
Loss%   = Loss / CP x 100
Discount% = Discount / MP x 100

Useful conversions

If profit is p%:

SP = CP x (100 + p) / 100

If discount is d%:

SP = MP x (100 - d) / 100

Do not use marked price as the denominator for profit percentage. Profit percentage is based on cost price.

11. Simple and Compound Interest

Simple interest

SI = P x R x T / 100
Amount = P + SI

Compound interest

Amount = P x (1 + R/100)^T
CI = Amount - P

For two years, the difference between compound and simple interest is:

P x (R/100)^2

Use this shortcut only when the rate and time conditions match.

12. Time, Speed and Distance

Distance = Speed x Time
Speed = Distance / Time
Time = Distance / Speed

Unit conversions

1 m/s = 18/5 km/h
1 km/h = 5/18 m/s

Relative speed

  • Opposite directions: add speeds.
  • Same direction: subtract speeds.

Average speed

For equal distances at speeds a and b:

Average speed = 2ab / (a + b)

Do not use the ordinary average unless the times or conditions justify it.

13. Time and Work

If A completes work in 12 days:

A's one-day work = 1/12

If A and B work together:

Combined rate = A's rate + B's rate

LCM shortcut

For workers taking 12 and 18 days, assume total work is LCM(12,18) = 36 units:

A rate = 3 units/day
B rate = 2 units/day
Together = 5 units/day
Time = 36/5 days

Use this method instead of repeatedly adding fractions.

14. Mixture and Allegation

For two ingredients with values A and B producing mean M:

Quantity of cheaper : quantity of dearer
= (dearer - mean) : (mean - cheaper)

Check that the mean lies between the two ingredient values. If it does not, the setup is wrong.

15. Data Interpretation: How to Choose a Set

Take a DI set when

  • The table or graph is readable.
  • At least two questions are direct.
  • Values are clean or can be simplified by ratios.
  • There are no too many missing values.

Skip temporarily when

  • Every question requires a different long calculation.
  • Percentages are awkward and options are close.
  • The chart requires extensive reading before the first calculation.
  • You cannot identify the base of a percentage comparison.

DI solving sequence

  1. Read the title, units and legend.
  2. Read all five questions before calculating.
  3. Identify common totals or repeated values.
  4. Solve direct questions first.
  5. Reuse calculated values for later questions.
  6. Use approximation when options permit.

Percentage-base warning

“A is what percent of B” means:

A / B x 100

The comparison base is B, not A.

16. Arithmetic Selection Framework

Take an arithmetic question now if:

  • You can write the equation in under 15 seconds.
  • The numbers are clean or options help.
  • The question has one main concept.
  • You can estimate the answer range.

Return later if:

  • The question contains a long story with several unknowns.
  • You need to try multiple cases.
  • The first equation is not clear.
  • The answer may require an exact fraction or lengthy decimal calculation.

Equation habit

Translate words before calculating:

of          -> multiplication
per         -> division or rate
more than   -> addition
less than   -> subtraction
is to       -> ratio
remaining  -> total - used

17. Calculation-Speed Program

Mental facts to memorize

  • Squares from 1 to 30.
  • Cubes from 1 to 15.
  • Fraction-percentage conversions.
  • Multiplication tables through 20.
  • Common percentage changes and ratio equivalents.

Daily timed drills

Drill A: 10 simplifications in 5 minutes
Drill B: 10 approximations in 5 minutes
Drill C: 10 quadratic comparisons in 5 minutes
Drill D: 5 number series in 3 minutes
Drill E: 10 percentage/ratio questions in 8 minutes

Do not only record the score. Record the reason for each delay:

F = formula not known
C = calculation slow
R = reading or setup slow
E = careless error
S = poor question selection

The largest category should determine the next drill.

18. Accuracy Rules That Save Marks

  • Read the question’s final demand before calculating.
  • Write units for time, distance, money and percentage.
  • Check whether the answer should be greater or smaller before selecting an option.
  • Recheck negative signs and subtraction.
  • Do not round in the middle of an exact calculation unless the options allow it.
  • For ratio questions, verify that all parts add to the stated total.
  • For work questions, verify that the combined time is less than each individual time.
  • For percentage questions, identify the denominator explicitly.

20. Final Section Checklist

Before submitting Quant answers, ask:

  • Did I complete all direct short questions?
  • Did I spend too long on one DI set or arithmetic problem?
  • Did I use approximation where the options allowed it?
  • Did I avoid guesses with no elimination basis?
  • Did I check units, signs, denominators and percentage bases?
  • Did I leave enough time to review marked answers?

The winning habit is disciplined selection. Faster calculation helps, but refusing slow questions at the correct moment protects more marks than forcing one additional difficult problem.
