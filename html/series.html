Number Series for SBI PO / IBPS PO Prelims

1. What to Expect

Number series is a high-return Quant topic because the question format is short, but the pattern must be recognized quickly. In the supplied papers, wrong-number series and consecutive-number series were directly visible. Across the source-based material and recurring bank-exam patterns, the main forms are:

  • Missing number series
  • Wrong number series
  • Double pattern or mixed series
  • Square/cube-based series
  • Prime, arithmetic and geometric series

The practice questions below are arranged as five questions per type. Questions marked Source-based are taken from or closely transcribed from the supplied PDFs. Questions marked Pattern practice are original questions built around the same recurring formats and difficulty.

2. The 20-Second Pattern Scan

Check patterns in this order:

  1. First differences.
  2. Second differences.
  3. Multiplication or division.
  4. Multiplication followed by addition/subtraction.
  5. Alternating odd/even positions.
  6. Squares, cubes and prime numbers.
Increasing slowly       -> differences, squares or primes
Increasing rapidly      -> multiplication or cubes
Alternating movement    -> double pattern
One suspicious term     -> wrong-number check
One blank               -> missing-term check

Time rule

  • Missing series: usually give it 30-40 seconds.
  • Wrong series: give it 30-45 seconds; check from the beginning.
  • Double or mixed series: give it 40-50 seconds.
  • If no structure appears after one systematic scan, skip and return later.

3. Core Shortcuts

Difference pattern

5, 9, 15, 23, 33
Differences: +4, +6, +8, +10

Multiplication pattern

3, 7, 15, 31
Rule: x2 + 1

Alternating pattern

Separate positions:

2, 5, 4, 10, 6, 15, 8, ?
Odd positions:  2, 4, 6, 8
Even positions: 5, 10, 15, ?

Square/cube recognition

1, 4, 9, 16, 25 -> squares
1, 8, 27, 64, 125 -> cubes

Wrong-number method

Do not fit the pattern around the suspicious number only. Start at the first term and verify every operation until the sequence breaks.

4. Missing Number Series - 5 Questions

Q1 - Pattern practice

Find the missing term:

5, 9, 17, 33, 65, ?

Answer: 129

Solution: Each term is multiplied by 2 and 1 is subtracted.

5 x 2 - 1 = 9
9 x 2 - 1 = 17
65 x 2 - 1 = 129

Q2 - Pattern practice

3, 8, 18, 38, 78, ?

Answer: 158

Solution: x2 + 2.

Q3 - Pattern practice

4, 9, 19, 39, 79, ?

Answer: 159

Solution: x2 + 1.

Q4 - Pattern practice

2, 6, 12, 20, 30, ?

Answer: 42

Solution: Differences are +4, +6, +8, +10, +12.

Q5 - Pattern practice

1, 4, 10, 22, 46, ?

Answer: 94

Solution: x2 + 2.

Missing-series speed lesson

First inspect the difference. If the numbers grow rapidly, check x2, x3, x2 +/- constant before trying complicated differences.

5. Wrong Number Series - 5 Questions

Q6 - Source-based: supplied PDF 1, Q59

Find the wrong term:

8, 21, 41, 72, 116, 175, 257

Answer: 257

Solution: The expected differences are +13, +20, +29, +40, +53, +68, whose increments do not give a clean progression around the last term. This source question is best treated as a pattern-check question; verify the original PDF answer key before using it as a benchmark because extracted source text alone does not show the official explanation.

Q7 - Source-based: supplied PDF 1, Q60

5040, 720, 120, 24, 8, 2

Answer: 8

Solution: The intended division pattern is:

5040 / 7 = 720
720 / 6 = 120
120 / 5 = 24
24 / 4 = 6
6 / 3 = 2

Therefore 8 is wrong; it should be 6.

Q8 - Source-based: supplied PDF 3, Q45

14, 16, 12, 18, 10, 22, 8

Answer: 16

Solution: Separate odd and even positions:

Odd positions: 14, 12, 10, 8       (-2)
Even positions should be: 20, 18, 16

The second term should be 20, so 16 is wrong.

Q9 - Source-based: supplied PDF 3, Q46

204800, 6400, 400, 50, 12.5, 6.5, 6.25

Answer: 6.5

Solution: Divide successively by 32, 16, 8, 4, 2:

204800 / 32 = 6400
6400 / 16 = 400
400 / 8 = 50
50 / 4 = 12.5
12.5 / 2 = 6.25

The term 6.5 is wrong.

Q10 - Source-based: supplied PDF 4, Q53

380, 381, 387, 413, 476, 600, 815

Answer: 600

Solution: Expected differences are consecutive squares:

+1, +6, +26, +63, +124, +215

The sequence is not cleanly recoverable from the extracted text alone. Treat this as a source-based wrong-number practice item and verify the original answer key before memorizing the intended rule. The exam lesson is to test the first difference and second difference before guessing.

Wrong-series speed lesson

Check whether one term is breaking:

  1. A division or multiplication chain.
  2. A difference chain.
  3. An alternating chain.
  4. A square/cube or prime-based chain.

Never choose the visually unusual number without checking the operation before and after it.

6. Double Pattern / Mixed Series - 5 Questions

Q11 - Pattern practice

2, 5, 4, 10, 6, 15, 8, ?

Answer: 20

Solution: Odd positions are 2, 4, 6, 8; even positions are 5, 10, 15, 20.

Q12 - Pattern practice

3, 6, 8, 16, 13, 26, 18, ?

Answer: 52

Solution: Odd positions add 5: 3, 8, 13, 18. Even positions multiply by 2: 6, 16, 26, 52.

Q13 - Pattern practice

1, 4, 3, 9, 5, 16, 7, 25, ?

Answer: 9

Solution: Odd positions are consecutive odd numbers. Even positions are 2^2, 3^2, 4^2, 5^2.

Q14 - Pattern practice

10, 12, 24, 27, 54, 58, 116, ?

Answer: 121

Solution: Alternate +2, x2, +3, x2, +4, x2, +5.

Q15 - Pattern practice

8, 16, 13, 26, 23, 46, 43, ?

Answer: 86

Solution: Alternate x2, -3.

Double-pattern speed lesson

When the sequence rises and falls irregularly, immediately split odd and even terms. This is usually faster than trying to force one rule across every term.

7. Square/Cube-Based Series - 5 Questions

Q16 - Pattern practice

2, 5, 10, 17, 26, ?

Answer: 37

Solution: 1^2 + 1, 2^2 + 1, 3^2 + 1...; next is 6^2 + 1 = 37.

Q17 - Pattern practice

1, 8, 27, 64, 125, ?

Answer: 216

Solution: Consecutive cubes: 1^3, 2^3, 3^3, 4^3, 5^3, 6^3.

Q18 - Pattern practice

4, 9, 16, 25, 36, ?

Answer: 49

Solution: Consecutive squares from 2^2 to 7^2.

Q19 - Pattern practice

3, 10, 29, 66, 127, ?

Answer: 218

Solution: 1^3 + 2, 2^3 + 2, 3^3 + 2...; next is 6^3 + 2 = 218.

Q20 - Pattern practice

2, 9, 28, 65, 126, ?

Answer: 217

Solution: 1^3 + 1, 2^3 + 1, 3^3 + 1...; next is 6^3 + 1 = 217.

Square/cube speed lesson

If differences are 3, 5, 7, 9, suspect consecutive squares. If differences grow rapidly, test cubes before trying mixed multiplication.

8. Prime, Arithmetic and Geometric Series - 5 Questions

Q21 - Prime series

2, 3, 5, 7, 11, ?

Answer: 13

Solution: Consecutive prime numbers.

Q22 - Arithmetic series

5, 8, 11, 14, 17, ?

Answer: 20

Solution: Common difference +3.

Q23 - Geometric series

3, 6, 12, 24, 48, ?

Answer: 96

Solution: Common ratio x2.

Q24 - Descending geometric series

81, 27, 9, 3, ?

Answer: 1

Solution: Divide by 3 each time.

Q25 - Arithmetic difference series

7, 10, 15, 22, 31, ?

Answer: 42

Solution: Differences are +3, +5, +7, +9, +11.

Prime/AP/GP speed lesson

Check a constant difference before a sophisticated rule. For rapidly growing terms, check a constant ratio. For small irregular increases, test odd-number or prime-number differences.

The recurring lesson is that the exam rewards simple operations checked in sequence: differences, division/multiplication, alternating terms and familiar powers.

10. Exam Attempt Strategy

First scan

  • Take a missing series if the first two operations are obvious.
  • Take a wrong series only when a clean rule appears quickly.
  • Take a double series after splitting odd and even positions.
  • Take square/cube series when the values visibly resemble powers.

Skip conditions

  • No pattern after 30-45 seconds.
  • More than one plausible rule with no option-based confirmation.
  • A wrong series requires forcing an irregular pattern.
  • You are spending time calculating rather than recognizing.

Accuracy check

Before marking:

  • Verify the rule from the first term.
  • Check the answer against at least two transitions.
  • For wrong series, confirm what the term should be.
  • For double series, verify both subsequences.

11. One-Page Recognition Flow

Are terms changing by a small amount?
  Yes -> check first and second differences
  No  -> check multiplication/division

Does the pattern alternate?
  Yes -> split odd and even positions
  No  -> continue with one-rule checks

Do terms resemble powers?
  Yes -> test squares, cubes or power +/- constant
  No  -> test primes and mixed operations

No rule in 30-45 seconds?
  Skip and return later

13. Low-Hanging Fruits from the Model Tests

The model tests repeatedly use five fast patterns: changing differences, division chains, alternating positions, multiply-then-add, and prime/square differences. Cover these before complicated series.

A. Changing differences

124.5, 120, 111, 97.5, ?, 57

Differences: -4.5, -9, -13.5, -18, -22.5. Answer: 79.5.

MT-3 Q55

500, 498, 492, 480, ?, 430

Subtract 2, 6, 12, 20, 30. Answer: 460.

MT-8 Q66

250, 235, 205, 160, ?

Differences: -15, -30, -45, -60. Answer: 100.

MT-24 Q52

25, 35, 49, 67, 89, ?

Differences: +10, +14, +18, +22, +26. Answer: 115.

MT-24 Q53

10, 11, 15, 24, 40, ?

Differences are consecutive squares: +1, +4, +9, +16, +25. Answer: 65.

B. Division and multiplication chains

MT-3 Q54

446, 222, 110, 54, 26, ?

Rule: (previous - 2) / 2. Therefore (26 - 2)/2 = 12. Answer: 12.

MT-24 Q54

11, 9, 15, 41, 159, ?

Rule: x1-2, x2-3, x3-4, x4-5, x5-6. Answer: 789.

MT-24 Q55

18, 9, 9, 18, 72, ?

Multipliers: 1/2, x1, x2, x4, x8. Answer: 576.

MT-20 Q47

5, 30, 124, 375, 752, ?

Use the decreasing multiplier pattern: 5x1+25, 30x4+4, 124x3+3, 375x2+2, then 752x1+1. Answer: 753.

20, ?, 29, 40, 58

Differences are +3, +6, +11, +18; their increases are +3, +5, +7. Answer: 23.

MT-8 Q68

5, 7, 17, 55, 225, ?

Rule: x1+2, x2+3, x3+4, x4+5, x5+6. Answer: 1131.

MT-9 Q55 - wrong number

9, 73, 17, 65, 25, 56, 33

Odd terms: 9,17,25,33 (+8); even terms: 73,65,57 (-8). Wrong number: 56; it should be 57.

D. Multiply-then-add

3, 24, 125, 504, ?, 3032

The intended operations are 3x1+21, 24x5+5, 125x4+4, 504x3+3, 1515x2+2. Answer: 1515.

4, 18, 46, 102, 214, 437, 886

E. Prime and alternating division

15, 38, 67, 98, 135, 175, 219

Expected differences: +23, +29, +31, +37, +41, +43. Wrong number: 175; it should be 176.

MT-9 Q52 - wrong number

84, 42, 126, 63, 189, 94, 283.5

Alternate /2, x3; 189/2 = 94.5, and 94.5x3 = 283.5. Wrong number: 94.

14. Most Important Models to Cover First

Must-cover

  1. Difference increasing by a constant amount.
  2. x2 +/- a or x3 +/- a.
  3. Alternating odd/even series.
  4. Division chains.
  5. Wrong-number series with one broken transition.

Cover next

  1. Squares/cubes with a constant adjustment.
  2. Prime-number differences.
  3. AP and GP.
  4. Decimal series with regular subtraction.
  5. Consecutive even/odd double series.

Leave for last

  • Several plausible rules with no option-based confirmation.
  • Long decimals with no clear scale.
  • A rule that works only after ignoring two or more terms.

15. Five-Minute Revision Drill

Minute 1: 2 difference series
Minute 2: 2 x2/x3 or division series
Minute 3: 2 alternating series
Minute 4: 2 wrong-number series
Minute 5: 1 square/cube or prime series

Label mistakes: D differences, M multiplication, A alternating, P powers/primes, C calculation, T time. Target the most frequent code in the next drill.
