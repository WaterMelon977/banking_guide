SBI PO / IBPS PO Prelims Strategy — Safe Cutoff Plan

1. Your Current Position

Your reported mock score is approximately 55:

English       34
Quant         10
Reasoning     11
Total         55

Your fastest route to a safer score is not to master every topic. It is to:

  1. Protect English at 30+ with high accuracy.
  2. Raise Quant through short, repeatable topics and faster calculations.
  3. Raise Reasoning by taking quick individual questions before attempting only manageable puzzles.
  4. Reduce negative marks and time lost on difficult sets.

2. Exam Reality and Important Caveat

Recent official patterns use three separately timed 20-minute sections. The SBI PO 2025 official information handout listed English Language, Quantitative Aptitude and Reasoning Ability, with 100 questions in 1 hour and 20 minutes per section. The IBPS PO/MT XV notification was issued for vacancies of 2026-27; IBPS has also released the current PO/MT XVI notification page, so verify the latest notification before each attempt.

Official references:

  • SBI PO 2025 official information handout
  • IBPS PO/MT XV official notification
  • IBPS PO/MT XVI official updates

Do not prepare from a remembered pattern if the latest notification changes the number of questions, marks, timing, negative marking or cutoff rules.

3. The Safe-Cutoff Philosophy

Your objective is a reliable net score, not maximum attempts.

Net score = marks from correct answers - penalty from wrong answers

Use three rules:

  • Attempt a question when the method is clear and the expected accuracy is high.
  • Skip questions that require a fresh case setup, long calculation or repeated rereading.
  • Never allow one difficult puzzle or DI set to consume the section.

A practical improvement path is:

Current:       55
First target:  60-65 with better accuracy
Safer target:  70+ when the paper is moderate

Exact cutoffs vary by year, category, shift and vacancies. Use the latest previous-year cutoff data only as a reference, not a guarantee.

4. Section Order and Time Control

Because the sections are separately timed, you cannot borrow unused English time for Quant or Reasoning. Enter each section with a fixed first-pass and second-pass plan.

English: 20 minutes

First pass:  vocabulary, fillers, error spotting, easy rearrangement
Second pass: cloze test and manageable RC questions
Final pass: doubtful grammar and inference questions

Quant: 20 minutes

First pass:  simplification, approximation, quadratic, number series
Second pass: easy arithmetic and direct DI questions
Final pass: selected word problems only

Reasoning: 20 minutes

First pass:  inequality, syllogism, direction, blood relation, coding, ranking
Second pass: easy seating or floor/box puzzle
Final pass: remaining individual questions; abandon a slow puzzle

Do not begin with a puzzle merely because it has many questions. Begin with the quickest marks available.

5. English Language: What to Look Out For

English is currently your strongest section. Treat it as a score-protection section.

Priority 1: Direct and low-risk questions

Take these first when the wording is clear:

  • Error spotting with a visible subject-verb, tense, article, preposition or pronoun issue.
  • Fillers where one option fits grammar and meaning immediately.
  • Phrase replacement when only one option preserves both grammar and meaning.
  • Word usage or vocabulary questions when you know the word confidently.
  • Easy sentence rearrangement with a clear opening sentence and mandatory pair.

Priority 2: Reading comprehension

Do not automatically skip RC. First inspect its length and question type.

  • Read the questions quickly before the passage if the passage is factual.
  • Locate answers by keywords, but read the surrounding sentence.
  • For inference questions, choose what is supported, not what is merely plausible.
  • Avoid spending time on one vocabulary-in-context question.
  • If the passage is dense and the questions are mostly inference-based, take the other English questions first.

Priority 3: Cloze test

Use grammar first, then context:

  1. Read the whole sentence around the blank.
  2. Eliminate options with the wrong part of speech.
  3. Check collocation and logical meaning.
  4. Re-read the paragraph after filling the blank.

English speed rules

  • Do not debate two options for more than 30–40 seconds unless the question is worth returning to.
  • In error spotting, inspect the sentence in this order: subject-verb, tense, pronoun, modifier, preposition, article and parallelism.
  • In rearrangement, find the opening sentence, mandatory pairs and pronoun references before ordering the remaining sentences.
  • Aim for high accuracy rather than attempting every question.

6. Quantitative Aptitude: Low-Hanging Fruits

Your Quant score is the biggest immediate opportunity. Build a dependable set of short topics before chasing difficult arithmetic.

Take these first

  1. Simplification and approximation: Use fraction equivalents, percentage anchors and order of operations.
  2. Quadratic equations: Compare roots using signs and approximate values; avoid long exact calculations.
  3. Number series: Check first difference, second difference, multiplication/addition pattern and alternating pattern.
  4. Easy data interpretation: Take a set only when the table or chart is readable and at least two questions look direct.
  5. Basic arithmetic: Percentage, ratio, average, profit and loss, simple interest, time and work, and speed-distance.

Calculation-speed rules

  • Memorize squares at least through 30, cubes through 15 and fraction-percentage equivalents.
  • Use 1/2 = 50%, 1/3 = 33.33%, 1/4 = 25%, 1/5 = 20%, 1/8 = 12.5%, 1/10 = 10%.
  • Cancel before multiplying fractions.
  • Estimate before calculating; if options are far apart, approximation is enough.
  • Keep rough work in aligned columns. Untidy arithmetic creates avoidable errors.
  • Do not calculate a long expression exactly if the options can be separated by a quick estimate.

Arithmetic question selection

Take a word problem when:

  • The data is direct and the formula is obvious.
  • The question has small numbers or clean ratios.
  • You can identify the first two steps immediately.

Skip temporarily when:

  • There are several cases or unknowns.
  • The calculation includes awkward decimals and no useful options.
  • You have reread the question twice without forming an equation.

Topics to postpone initially

Leave these for later unless they are unusually direct:

  • Long mixture and allegation questions.
  • Multi-step permutation/probability.
  • Difficult partnerships.
  • Complex time and work with changing efficiency.
  • DI sets requiring many percentage conversions.

7. Reasoning: What to Pick First

Your priority is to collect individual-question marks before deciding whether a puzzle is worth entering.

First-pass order

  1. Inequality.
  2. Syllogism with direct conclusions.
  3. Direction and distance.
  4. Blood relations.
  5. Coding-decoding with a short rule.
  6. Ranking and order.
  7. Alphanumeric or letter-number series.
  8. Easy statement-conclusion or comparison questions.

These topics usually require less setup than a full puzzle and can be completed quickly when familiar.

Puzzle selection rule

Spend 20–30 seconds scanning each puzzle. Choose it only if:

  • At least one clue gives a fixed position or a strong pair.
  • The number of entities is manageable.
  • The diagram can be drawn quickly.
  • You can see a likely starting clue.
  • The questions appear to use the same arrangement.

Avoid a puzzle initially when:

  • It has many conditional cases and no fixed clue.
  • The wording is ambiguous.
  • You are already spending time drawing without placing anyone.
  • The first question requires completing the entire puzzle.

Faster puzzle method

  • Draw the smallest useful diagram: slots for seating, floors for building puzzles, columns for attributes.
  • Start with direct, immediate and extreme clues.
  • Convert clues into symbols: A > B, A immediately above B, A != 3.
  • Build chains instead of placing people one at a time.
  • Use case branches only when both cases remain possible.
  • Reject a branch as soon as one clue fails.
  • Once the arrangement answers the asked questions, stop solving unused cells.

8. Mock-Test Method

Take mocks as measurement tools, not as a daily score judgment.

Before the mock

  • Use the same rough-sheet style each time.
  • Decide your first-pass order in each section.
  • Set an attempt ceiling for doubtful questions.

During the mock

Record mentally or on the rough sheet:

  • Questions skipped because they were too long.
  • Questions where calculation, not concept, caused delay.
  • Puzzles abandoned and the time spent before abandoning them.
  • Questions answered with low confidence.

After the mock

Do not only check the score. Classify every incorrect or skipped question:

K = Did not know the concept
C = Concept known, calculation slow
M = Misread the question
S = Selected a bad question / spent too long
G = Guessed or answered with low confidence

Your next practice session should target the largest category, not the most interesting topic.

9. Practical Speed-Improvement Drills

Quant

  • Do short timed sets of 10 simplification or approximation questions.
  • Practise 20 mental percentage/fraction conversions.
  • Redo missed questions without looking at the solution, but enforce a time limit.
  • Track calculation errors separately from concept errors.

Reasoning

  • Solve 10 inequality and syllogism questions in a short burst.
  • Draw one puzzle diagram from clues without attempting the questions first.
  • Practise abandoning a puzzle after a fixed scan time.
  • Redo the same puzzle later and compare diagram speed, not just correctness.

English

  • Complete short grammar sets with an accuracy target.
  • Read one editorial or banking article and write its main idea in one sentence.
  • Review every unfamiliar word from mocks in a sentence, not as an isolated list.
  • Practise locating RC evidence rather than rereading the entire passage.

10. The Exam-Day Playbook

English

Secure the direct grammar, fillers and vocabulary questions first. Move to RC or cloze only after collecting the visible marks.

Quant

Start with simplification, approximation, quadratic and series. Then take one clean DI or direct arithmetic question. Do not let a long word problem consume the section.

Reasoning

Finish quick individual questions first. Select the puzzle with the clearest entry point. If the diagram is not progressing, leave it and take the next individual questions.

Final rules

  • Do not chase a question because you have already spent time on it.
  • Do not guess when two options remain equally likely.
  • Protect accuracy in English.
  • Improve Quant and Reasoning by selection, calculation and diagram speed.
  • Review the latest official notification before the exam because pattern details can change.
